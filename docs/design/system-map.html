Nudge: how it works

System map of the final judge build, 0.4.5 (integration, Sun Oct 18, 2026): Practice + Assist. Conversation (Step 3) is built but off in this build. First drawn by Sam (AI lane), updated by ORCH.

Nudge is a quiet word-finding aid for people with aphasia, built for the Even G2 smart glasses. When a word won't come in a conversation, a tap shows the likeliest word letters first: s _ _ _ _, then s u n _ _, then sunny, so the person still says it themselves. There are three modes: Practice (fill in the missing word, with a picture → letters → word cue ladder), Assist (tap for help mid-conversation) and Conversation (Assist plus automatic hints after a stall; experimental, off in the judge build).

  • Quiet: the display stays blank until help is wanted
  • Private: audio is never stored; the word bank stays on the phone
  • Safe keys: API keys live only on the server, never in the app
  • Degrades, never dies: no network or model? Fall back to the word bank

How each mode works

Step 1 · Practice

Rebuild word retrieval with fill-in-the-blank prompts.

  1. Glasses show a sentence with a gap: "You drink from a ____."
  2. The glasses mic opens and stays warm 10 s between prompts. Local VAD waits for speech (≥ 12 dB over the room).
  3. 1.2 s of silence ends the attempt (max 8 s). Only the speech is uploaded.
  4. A pre-minted single-use token plus the audio go to Scribe batch, which returns text in about 0.3 s. The target is never sent as a hint, so "cop" stays "cop".
  5. A fuzzy matcher decides ✓ or ✗.
  6. A tap, or a wrong answer, climbs the cue ladder: picture (or mouth shape) → letters (c_p) → the whole word. Short background noise that doesn't match (under 250 ms) is ignored; a correct answer always counts.
  7. After 10 prompts the session is saved on the phone and shown in the companion's Step 1 log.

Step 2 · Assist

Help on request during a real conversation. The core of the judge build.

  1. The display stays blank while a rolling transcript builds up from short batch segments (the realtime WebSocket gets nothing on the device). Segments in a non-English script, or short and quiet (partner fragments), are dropped.
  2. Suggestions are prefetched in the background, at most 1 per 5 s to stay inside Gemini's free tier (15/min). When a sentence ends like a stall ("…into the", "um"), prediction starts at once.
  3. The user taps. The segment being spoken is transcribed immediately (no waiting for a pause), then Gemini ranks the top 3 (about 1.0 s through our proxy) or the cached prefetch answers. Tap to letters: median ≈ 1.5 s in the simulator; the device felt slower at G2.
  4. Letters first: s _ _ _ _, a tap shows s u n _ _, another shows sunny. A swipe moves to the next guess. No pictures or mouth shapes here.
  5. Saying the word clears the display. A double-tap hides it; it also clears after 20 s.
  6. If the model is slow (over 1.5 s), down or rate-limited, suggestions come from the word bank, but only words that fit the conversation; otherwise "No suggestion yet".

Step 3 · Conversation (experimental, off in 0.4.5)

Help without asking: Step 2 plus a stall detector.

  1. The detector watches silence and how the last phrase ended: "…pour my tea into the", "take my, um", "a cop, no, … a cuh".
  2. After 1.5 s of silence following an unfinished phrase, Step 3 taps for the user. It never interrupts a hint already showing, waits 10 s between hints (max 3/min), and a swipe on the blank display pauses it.
  3. On the test conversations: 0 false hints in 57 normal pauses, 11 of 12 stalls caught.
  4. On the G2 (gate G3): 4 of 8 automatic hints were real stalls, but partner fragments caused false ones, so it ships off.
  5. Measured next step: on the glasses mic the wearer reads −9 to −15 dB and the partner −24 to −30 dB, so a −20 dB split can tell who is speaking.

Limits today

  • Latency: about 1.5 s from tap to letters in the simulator. The realtime transcript would save about a second, but it gets nothing from a packaged build on the G2 (cause unconfirmed), so Steps 2–3 use batch segments.
  • Who is talking: the glasses mic hears the partner too. Speaker separation by loudness is measured but not built.
  • Gemini free tier: 15 requests a minute plus a daily cap. The app throttles and falls back to the word bank; billing removes the cap.
  • Not clinically validated: a hackathon prototype, tested by the team on one pair of glasses.
